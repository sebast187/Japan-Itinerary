The basics

Your base: District 10

Bamboo Saigon Hotel sits on Sư Vạn Hạnh Street, District 10 — not District 1. That matters for pacing: most of the classic D1 sights (War Remnants Museum, Notre-Dame, Ben Thanh, Bui Vien) are a genuine 15–25 minute Grab away, not a stroll. The upside: you're already close to Ho Thị Kỳ Flower Market and well-positioned for Cholon (District 5 is right next door), which is why those two show up as your easiest nights.

Data

eSIM (Airalo/Saily/Holafly, ~US$4 for a small plan up to ~US$20–32 for 20GB/30 days) if you want to sort it from the room. Otherwise find an actual Viettel, Mobifone or VinaPhone store — convenience stores don't sell SIMs anymore. In-store tourist SIMs run roughly 90,000–220,000 VND for 15–30 days.

Cash & cards

Cards work at malls, hotels and mid-range restaurants; street food, markets and bia hơi corners are cash-only. Rough mental-math rates: ≈26,000 VND = US$1, ≈18,300 VND = A$1 (moves a little day to day).

Apps worth having

Grab (rides + food delivery, the default here) · Google Maps with District 1 and District 10 downloaded offline · Google Translate camera mode for menus · a currency converter for quick VND checks.

02
Tue 22 Sep · TODAY

Old Saigon, on foot

The original plan stacked five stops into one day — palace, museum, cathedral, post office, market. We kept the one that's genuinely worth the time and turned the rest into a walk, not a checklist. It's a District 1/3 day, so budget ~20 min each way from the hotel.

Morning

War Remnants Museum 40,000 VND · ~2h

28 Võ Văn Tần, District 3. It opens at 7:30 — worth getting there close to that, both for the heat and the crowds. It tells the war entirely from the Vietnamese side with no softening, including a heavy Agent Orange section, so go in braced for that. It's the one "museum day" we kept, because it's not really comparable to a normal museum visit.

Want one more stop before lunch?

Three options depending on mood: Independence Palace (40,000 VND, ~1.5h) for the full 1960s time-capsule of the old South Vietnamese presidency, tanks included; the Secret War Bunker at 287/70 Nguyễn Đình Chiểu — a Viet Cong weapons cache hidden under an ordinary house, small, strange, almost no tourists know about it; or Jade Emperor Pagoda (73 Mai Thị Lựu, Đa Kao — free/donation), a short Grab away, all carved wood, incense, and resident tortoises.

Midday

Notre-Dame + Central Post Office free · 20–30 min

Heads up: the cathedral's been under restoration since 2017 and isn't fully finished until 2027, so don't expect the postcard shot — parts are still scaffolded. Still worth a walk past for the architecture. The Post Office next door more than makes up for it: free to wander, genuinely stunning colonial interior, and you can mail an actual postcard home from the same counters that have been there since the French built it.

Lunch: cơm tấm nearby ~50k–70k VND

Broken rice with a grilled pork chop, a fried egg, pickled veg — the everyday lunch of half the city. Any place with plastic stools and a full room of locals mid-meal is doing it right.

Afternoon

42 Nguyễn Huệ — the "apartment cafe" building ~35k–70k VND / drink

An old apartment block a few floors up, now packed wall-to-wall with tiny independent cafes, plant shops and clothing racks. Not a museum, not a market — just a slightly maze-like building you climb, floor by floor, until you find a spot with a balcony over Nguyễn Huệ and a coffee worth the climb.

Evening

Ben Thanh — but only the night stalls ~40k–90k VND / dish

Skip the daytime market entirely — it's where the classic tourist markup lives (see the price cheat-sheet below). After 5pm, plastic tables spill out around the building's perimeter and it turns into a proper street-food court: grilled seafood, stir-fries cooked in front of you, cold beer.

Nightlife

Rex Hotel Rooftop Bar ~150k–300k VND / drink

141 Nguyễn Huệ — a five-minute walk from where you'll already be. One of Saigon's original rooftop bars: colonial-era hotel, an elevated garden terrace looking straight down Nguyễn Huệ toward City Hall lit up gold. Less see-and-be-seen than the newer towers, more old-Saigon institution. Good pick for night one out since you're already right there.

Want something cheaper, or more low-key

Turtle Lake (Hồ Con Rùa) — a roundabout that's become the go-to late hangout for university students. Plastic stools ring the edge, cheap drinks and snacks (~15,000–30,000 VND), zero tourist energy, good people-watching, no cover charge for the view.

Wed 23 Sep · TODAY

Underground, then unwind

Two tunnel sites exist and most big-bus tours default to the closer, busier one without telling you. Worth knowing before you book.

Morning – early afternoon

Cu Chi Tunnels — go to Ben Dược ~US$25–35 pp

Ben Dinh (closer, ~50km) is where the big 40-person coach tours go — widened tunnels, more infrastructure, more crowds. Ben Dược (~70km) gets three to four times fewer visitors, keeps the tunnels closer to their original cramped size, and adds a large memorial temple. A small-group tour (max ~10–12 people) that specifically goes to Ben Dược is the move.

Tighter budget, or short on time

Standard big-bus Ben Dinh tours run from ~US$12–20 including hotel pickup and entrance. Entrance alone (both sites) is 110,000–135,000 VND, so an independent Grab there and back (~350,000–400,000 VND one-way) plus tickets works too, if you don't want a guide.

If you're curious: most sites offer a shooting range add-on (AK-47/M16, priced per bullet, usually a 10-round minimum, roughly 400,000–600,000 VND all in). Purely optional, worth knowing it exists rather than getting surprise-upsold on site.

Nightlife

Bùi Viện Walking Street beer ~15k–25k VND

Let's be honest about what this is: the backpacker strip, beer towers, street performers, everyone a little too loud. It's genuinely fun for one night. Worth the ~20 min Grab back out from the hotel — this one isn't walkable from District 10. A beer tower runs ~200,000–300,000 VND and easily splits between you two.

If that's not really your scene

Pasteur Street Brewing Co (District 1) — Vietnamese-brewed craft beer, proper IPAs and stouts, a calmer, slightly older crowd. ~90,000–150,000 VND a pint.

04
Thu 24 Sep · TODAY

Mekong, without the shuffle

Quick expectation-set: the famous floating market (Cai Răng, near Can Tho) is far enough away that it genuinely needs an overnight trip. What's doable in a day from Saigon is My Tho and Bến Tre — canals, coconut groves, a sampan row through the water-coconut trees.

Full day

My Tho + Bến Tre — small-group tour ~US$35–45 pp

Depart ~7:30–8am, ~1.5h drive out. Vĩnh Tràng pagoda, a motorised boat past Dragon/Unicorn/Phoenix/Tortoise islets, then a hand-rowed sampan through Bến Tre's coconut canals, a village stop for honey tea and tropical fruit with live folk music, and lunch included. A "lesser-known" small-group version (capped around 10) skips the worst of the big-bus bottlenecks at the coconut-candy workshop stop.

Cheaper, pricier, or more active options

Budget: standard group-bus version, from ~US$20 pp. Private: your own guide and car, set your own pace, from ~US$70–100+ pp. More active: Mekong cycling tours through the back roads and villages exist too (~US$40–70 pp) — better fit if a full day of being ferried around isn't your speed.

Nightlife

Ho Thị Kỳ Night Market ~30k–70k VND / dish

This is the one that's actually close to you — about 5 minutes from the hotel. Open 6pm–midnight, District 10, and genuinely more locals than tourists: a flower-trading market by day that turns into food stalls and general night-market chaos after dark — banh mi, pho, grilled seafood, plus clothes and household stalls if you feel like browsing. After a full day being ferried around the Mekong, this is the low-effort, high-reward option — no Grab back into D1 required.

If you're properly wiped

Completely fair after a 7am-start day trip — grab something from the hotel's own restaurant or order Grab Food to the room and call it an early one. Tomorrow's a big day.

Fri 25 Sep · TODAY

Cholon, and the last big night

One geography fix from the original plan: Jade Emperor Pagoda is actually up in District 1, not Cholon — we moved it to day two. Cholon's own temple is Thiên Hậu, and it's a better fit here anyway. Bonus: District 5 borders District 10, so today's the easiest commute of the trip.

Morning – midday

Bình Tây Market free to wander

Cholon's real market — wholesale, working, loud with trolleys and trade rather than souvenir stalls. Good for browsing, better for photos, and there's decent cheap food in the streets immediately around it.

Thiên Hậu Pagoda free/donation

710 Nguyễn Trãi, District 5. Built by Cantonese traders around 1790 — small, dim, thick with spiralling incense coils hanging from the ceiling, dedicated to the goddess of seafarers. Cover shoulders and knees. Fifteen quiet minutes that feel a world away from Bùi Viện.

Evening

Last big dinner

You've earned an actual sit-down dinner tonight. Aim for a proper mid-range D1 restaurant (~250,000–500,000 VND per person for a main and a drink) rather than the biggest rooftop name you can find — the good mid-range Vietnamese places in D1/D3 consistently outcook the view-first restaurants for less money. Ask your hotel front desk for tonight's pick.

Nightlife

Landmark 81 — skip the deck, take the bar drink ~150k–300k VND

The SkyView observation deck runs roughly 350,000–800,000 VND depending on package, and the ticket counter is buried in the basement. Skip it: several bars and lounges up on the 70-something floors are walk-in, no ticket required — same view, a seat, air conditioning, for the price of one drink. It's a proper trip from District 10 though — figure ~30 min each way — so make it the one deliberate cross-town night of the trip, saved for last.

Don't want the trek on your last night

Bitexco Skydeck is in D1, lotus-bud shaped, cheaper at ~200,000–230,000 VND, and still a genuinely great view — closer if you'd rather not cross town after already being out all day.

Sat 26 Sep · TODAY

Wheels up

Flight's at 13:15. International departures at Tan Son Nhat want a 3-hour buffer, and District 10 to the airport is a real drive, not a hop.

Morning: one last one

Early breakfast — a final bánh mì or bowl of phở walking distance from the hotel is a better send-off than the buffet. Checkout by ~9am.

Getting to Tân Sơn Nhất Grab ~100k–150k VND

Leave the hotel by ~9:30am — that's roughly a 3-hour buffer before a 13:15 international departure, with District 10's slightly longer haul to the airport (~20–30 min depending on traffic) factored in. GrabCar runs ~100,000–150,000 VND; a metered Vinasun or Mai Linh taxi is ~150,000–250,000 VND and just as reliable if Grab's surging.

What to buy, and where

Where to actually buy things

SIM / eSIM

eSIM anytime via Airalo, Saily or Holafly (~US$4–20 depending on data). Physical SIM: an actual Viettel/Mobifone/VinaPhone store, not a convenience shop.

Online — app, anytimePhysical — carrier stores only

Souvenirs & lacquerware

Ben Thanh's daytime stalls mark up 200–300%; the outer-ring stalls with staff in light-blue shirts are fixed-price if you'd rather skip haggling entirely. Bình Tây Market (Cholon) has better prices if you're up for negotiating. For nicer, pricier pieces, the boutiques around Đồng Khởi St are worth a browse.

Physical only — half the fun is the haggle

Clothes / streetwear

Saigon Square (District 1) for cheap, semi-fixed-price fashion finds. Scattered vintage and independent shops through District 1/3 backstreets reward actual wandering more than any single "shopping street" does.

Physical — try things on, it varies by cut

Coffee beans to take home

Skip the airport souvenir rack. A proper bag from a specialty roaster or a Trung Nguyên shop runs ~80,000–150,000 VND and actually tastes like what you drank all week.

Physical — buy from a shop you've actually visited

Tours (Cu Chi, Mekong)

Book online a day ahead through Klook, GetYourGuide or Viator — transparent pricing, real reviews, exact itinerary before you show up. A hotel tour desk works fine too if the operator has visible reviews; a random street tout the morning-of is the one place worth being cautious.

Five quick things

Before you head out each day

01

Crossing the street is a walk, not a sprint — steady pace, no stopping, no sudden moves. Bikes flow around you; they can't if you freeze.

02

Temples want shoulders and knees covered — Thiên Hậu and Jade Emperor both.

03

Only Vinasun (white/green) or Mai Linh (green) if flagging a taxi off the street instead of using the app.

04

Agree the price before getting on any motorbike taxi that isn't booked through Grab.

05

Bag in front, zipped in Ben Thanh and Bùi Viện specifically — the two most crowded spots on this itinerary.
